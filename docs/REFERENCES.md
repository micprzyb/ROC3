# Annotated bibliography

Sources consulted while designing the 3-class ROC surface / VUS. Grouped by what each one
contributes to the design in [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md).

## Foundational: the 3-class ROC surface exists and VUS means something

* **Scurfield, B.K. (1996).** *Multiple-event forced-choice tasks in the theory of signal
  detectability.* J. Math. Psych. 40(3), 253–269.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8979976/>
  The original generalisation of ROC analysis to `n ≥ 3` events. Shows performance in an
  `n`-event identification task is representable in `n!` ROC spaces of dimension `n`, with a
  hypersurface generated by sweeping the observer's decision criteria. Defines a
  criterion-free discriminability measure `D` from the hypervolumes.
  *Used for:* the legitimacy of "sweep the decision criteria, get a surface, take the volume".
  `D` itself was **rejected** for us (not on a [0,1] perfect-=-1 scale) — see [`IDEAS_LOG.md`](IDEAS_LOG.md) §B6.

* **Scurfield, B.K. (1998).** *Generalization of the theory of signal detectability to
  n-event m-dimensional forced-choice tasks.* J. Math. Psych.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9606159/>

* **Mossman, D. (1999).** *Three-way ROCs.* Medical Decision Making 19(1), 78–89.
  <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0272989X9901900110>
  **The key reference for our axes and our VUS.** Plots the ROC surface on 3-D coordinates;
  states that *"the volume under the ROC surface (VUS) equals the probability that test
  values will allow a decision maker to correctly sort a trio of items containing a
  randomly-selected member from each of three populations"*, with **VUS = 1/6 for an
  uninformative marker and 1 for a perfect one**.
  *Used for:* PLAN §3.3 (the 3AFC estimator) and the 1/6 chance level.

* **He, X. & Frey, E.C. (2008).** *The meaning and use of the volume under a three-class ROC
  surface (VUS).* IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 27(5), 577–588.
  <https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4359076/>
  Ties VUS to percent-correct in a three-class categorisation task, and clarifies what VUS
  does and does not summarise.

## Why the surface is 2-D (and why we restrict the rule family)

* **He, X., Gallas, B.D., Frey, E.C. (2010).** *Three-class ROC analysis — toward a general
  decision theoretic solution.* IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2821068/>
  **The reference that justifies our decision-rule family.** A 3-class problem has 9
  accuracy fractions with 3 constraints ⇒ **6 free parameters**, needing "a 5-D hypersurface
  in 6-D hyperspace". Under the **Equal Error Utility (EEU)** assumption
  (`U₃₁=U₂₁, U₃₂=U₁₂, U₁₃=U₂₃`) the decision structure has **2 degrees of freedom**, giving
  a **2-D surface in the 3-D sensitivity space `{T₁,T₂,T₃}`**, and *"the VUS value is
  equivalent to the percent correct in a three-class categorization procedure"*; random ⇒ 1/6.
  Also proves the 2-D surface uniquely determines the likelihood-ratio distributions
  (i.e. the EEU restriction loses less than critics assumed).
  *Used for:* PLAN §A5. Our weighted-argmax family `argmax_k w_k p_k(x)` **is** the
  EEU-Bayes family: with `U_{i|k} = u_k·1[i=k] + v_k·1[i≠k]`, expected utility of deciding
  `i` is `(u_i−v_i)p_i(x) + Σ_k v_k p_k(x)`, whose second term is independent of `i`.

* **Edwards, D.C. & Metz, C.E. (2012).** *The three-class ideal observer for univariate
  normal data: decision variable and ROC surface properties.* J. Math. Psych.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3496401/>
  Ideal observer uses two likelihood ratios `LR_k = p(x|π_k)/p(x|π_N)`; the decision-boundary
  lines intersect the LR curve in at most six places, so the "5-D" hypersurface actually has
  **at most 4 degrees of freedom** — it is *degenerate*.
  *Used for:* PLAN §A4 — the reason the full hypersurface is rejected, not merely inconvenient.

* **Edwards, D.C., Metz, C.E. et al.** *Restrictions on the three-class ideal observer's
  decision boundary lines*; *The hypervolume under the ROC hypersurface of near-guessing and
  near-perfect observers in N-class classification tasks.* IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging.
  Establishes the near-guessing HUM value `1/N!` and near-perfect behaviour.

## Estimation, ordinal case, inference

* **Nakas, C.T. & Yiannoutsos, C.T. (2004).** *Ordered multiple-class ROC analysis with
  continuous measurements.* Statistics in Medicine 23(22), 3437–3449.
  The ordered/ordinal mode: single marker, two cut-points, `VUS = P(X₁ < X₂ < X₃)`;
  population variance estimator for VUS.
  *Used for:* `roc3/ordinal.py` and the U-statistic variance in `roc3/inference.py`.

* **Nakas, C.T. (2007).** *ROC graphs for assessing the ability of a diagnostic marker to
  detect three disease classes with an umbrella ordering.* Biometrics.
  <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1541-0420.2006.00715.x>
  Non-monotone ("umbrella") orderings — a caution that the ordinal mode assumes a direction.

* **Nakas, C.T., Alonzo, T.A., Yiannoutsos, C.T. (2010, 2013).** Generalised Youden index for
  three-class problems and the associated optimal *pair* of cut-points.
  *Used for:* the `youden` criterion in `roc3/thresholds.py`.

* **Wang, Y. et al. (2024).** *Evaluating joint confidence region of hypervolume under ROC
  manifold and generalized Youden index.* Statistics in Medicine.
  <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sim.9998>
  Joint inference on (HUM, GYI) — the modern statistical treatment of exactly the two
  quantities we report together.

* **Bantis, L.E., Nakas, C.T., Reiser, B. et al. (2022).** *Construction of joint confidence
  spaces for the optimal true class fraction triplet in the ROC space using alternative
  biomarker cutoffs.* <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9642830/>
  Confidence regions for the operating point `(S₁,S₂,S₃)` itself — motivates the bootstrap
  CIs we attach to the selected operating point, not just to VUS.

* **VUROCS** (R, CRAN) — VUS for multi-class with variance/covariance estimators.
  **trinROC** (R, CRAN) — `emp.vus`, trinormal tests.
  **ClusROC** (R, CRAN, 2025) — ROC surface, VUS, and **three threshold-selection criteria:
  Generalized Youden Index (GYI), Closest to Perfection (CtP), Maximum Volume (MV)**.
  *Used for:* the menu of criteria in `roc3/thresholds.py`.
  **HUM** (R, CRAN) — hypervolume under the manifold for `k > 3`.

## Machine-learning-side multiclass AUC generalisations (our reference numbers)

* **Hand, D.J. & Till, R.J. (2001).** *A simple generalisation of the area under the ROC
  curve for multiple class classification problems.* Machine Learning 45, 171–186.
  The `M` measure = mean pairwise AUC. Scalar only; chance = 1/2. Reported as a comparator.

* **Ferri, C., Hernández-Orallo, J., Salido, M.A. (2003).** *Volume under the ROC surface for
  multi-class problems.* ECML 2003, LNCS 2837, 108–120.
  <https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-39857-8_12>
  Exact computation of the VUS polytope for multi-class problems, and evaluation of
  approximations; shows the geometry of "no classifier"/"best classifier" polytopes.

* **Kleiman, R. & Page, D. (2019).** *AUCμ: A performance metric for multi-class machine
  learning models.* ICML 2019.
  <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kleiman19a/kleiman19a.pdf>
  Partition-based multiclass AUC using the full probability vector; scalar, chance = 1/2.
  Reported as a comparator.

* **Landgrebe, T.C.W. & Duin, R.P.W. (2007).** *Approximating the multiclass ROC by pairwise
  analysis.* Pattern Recognition Letters 28(13), 1747–1758.
  **(2008).** *Efficient multiclass ROC approximation by decomposition via confusion matrix
  perturbation analysis.* IEEE TPAMI 30(5), 810–822.
  Shows the multiclass ROC often decomposes because some ROC dimensions are independent —
  the practical argument for reduced parameterisations like ours.

* **Giudici, P. et al. (2025).** *A multiclass ROC curve.* arXiv:2510.15670.
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15670v1>
  A recent alternative: ZCA-whiten the predicted probabilities, form a weighted
  TPR/FPR curve via a multidimensional Gini index, `AUC_agg = (G_agg+1)/2`. Explicitly
  motivated by the complaint that *"beyond three classes, the resulting hypersurface cannot
  be directly visualized"*. Confirms that for **exactly 3 classes** — our case — the surface
  is the right object.

## Computational geometry

* The down-set volume in §3.2 is the **hypervolume indicator** with reference point at the
  origin, standard in multi-objective optimisation (Zitzler & Thiele 1998; Fonseca, Paquete
  & López-Ibáñez 2006 for the `O(n log n)` 3-D dimension-sweep algorithm we implement).
